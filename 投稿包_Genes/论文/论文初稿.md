# Benchmarking a plant DNA foundation model against convolutional neural networks for interpretable gene expression prediction in *Arabidopsis thaliana*

## Abstract

**Background/Objectives:** Sequence-based gene expression prediction is central to plant regulatory genomics, but practitioners lack a shared benchmark for choosing between compact convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and plant DNA foundation models. We quantified this trade-off in *Arabidopsis thaliana* under matched data and evaluation protocols.

**Methods:** We compared a DeepSEA-style 1D-CNN with AgroNT (1B parameters; LoRA fine-tuning) on the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) high-versus-low expression task. Labels were derived from tissue-averaged expression across 56 tissues/samples. We assessed regional importance (DeepLift; sliding-window mutagenesis), motif recovery (TF-MoDISco/TOMTOM), natural flanking-region variant effects, zero-shot crop transfer, input-length ablation, and tissue-label concordance supporting the averaged-label design.

**Results:** AgroNT outperformed the CNN (accuracy 86.5% vs. 75.4%; AUROC 0.935 vs. 0.842; AUROC difference bootstrap 95% CI 0.083–0.104; McNemar *p* = 6.5×10⁻⁴⁷). CNN attributions ranked 5′UTR > 3′UTR > promoter > terminator; recovered motifs were enriched for DOF-family factors. UTR variants showed larger predicted effects than terminator or promoter variants. Single-tissue high/low labels agreed with the tissue-averaged label for a mean of 92.1% of genes (range 78.7–95.4%). The Arabidopsis CNN transferred to rice, maize, tomato, and soybean with AUROC 0.69–0.76 without fine-tuning.

**Conclusions:** Under tissue-averaged expression labels, AgroNT provides a large, statistically supported gain over a strong CNN baseline at moderate LoRA cost, with interpretable proximal cis-regulatory signal concentrated in UTRs.

**Keywords**: gene expression prediction; cis-regulatory code; DNA language model; AgroNT; convolutional neural network; *Arabidopsis thaliana*; interpretability; cross-species transfer

## 1. Introduction

Most of a plant genome is non-coding sequence that determines when, where, and at what level genes are expressed through cis-regulatory elements (CREs) in promoters, 5′/3′ untranslated regions (UTRs), and terminators [marand2023]. Sequence-to-expression models that recover this code can support genome-wide annotation of uncharacterized genes, prioritization of noncoding variants, and design of synthetic regulatory DNA [hu2023].

Deep learning has shown that proximal DNA sequence alone can predict transcript abundance. In plants, Peleke et al. trained interpretable CNNs that classify high-versus-low expression from gene-flanking sequence across four species with >80% accuracy and recovered known transcription-factor motifs by attribution [peleke2024]. Parallel work has introduced plant DNA foundation models—large language models pretrained on whole-genome sequence. AgroNT, a one-billion-parameter transformer pretrained on ~10.5 million sequences from 48 edible plant genomes, reported strong results across the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) [mendoza2024, pgb2024]. What remains unclear for applied regulatory genomics is not whether either architecture can work, but how large the gain of foundation-model fine-tuning is relative to a well-tuned CNN under identical splits, metrics, and compute constraints, and which sequence compartments drive that prediction.

Here we provide that comparison in *Arabidopsis thaliana*. Under matched data and evaluation protocols, we train (i) a DeepSEA-style 1D-CNN baseline and (ii) AgroNT fine-tuned with low-rank adaptation (LoRA). We then quantify regional importance and motif content with DeepLift and TF-MoDISco, score natural flanking-region variants for predicted expression effects, measure concordance between tissue-averaged and single-tissue labels, and test zero-shot transfer of the Arabidopsis CNN to rice, maize, tomato, and soybean. The contribution is a reproducible performance–cost reference for CNN-versus-foundation-model expression prediction in plants, together with an interpretability and transfer analysis under tissue-averaged expression labels.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Data

We used the *Arabidopsis thaliana* gene-expression task of the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) [pgb2024, mendoza2024]. Each gene is represented by a 6-kb genomic window centered on the gene, with an expression label derived from 56 tissue/sample RNA-sequencing measurements. The official split comprises 25,731 training, 3,401 validation, and 3,402 test genes, with no gene overlap across splits. Binary high/low labels were defined by the median of per-gene mean expression on the training set, yielding a balanced two-class problem. Because labels aggregate expression across 56 tissues/samples, the benchmark targets cis-regulatory determinants of overall (tissue-averaged) expression potential rather than organ- or tissue-specific regulatory programs.

In parallel, we built a structured gene-centric dataset for region-level interpretability. For each of 21,694 protein-coding *A. thaliana* genes (TAIR10/Araport11 annotation, Ensembl Plants release 59), we extracted the promoter (1 kb upstream of the transcription start site), 5′UTR (500 nt), 3′UTR (500 nt), and terminator (1 kb downstream of the transcription termination site) in transcriptional orientation, concatenated the four regions 5′→3′ with a 20-nt N-padding gap, and one-hot encoded the resulting ~3 kb sequence. Genes were assigned to training/validation/test splits by chromosome (chromosomes 5 and 3, 4, and 1 and 2, respectively) to reduce sequence-homology leakage between splits.

Parsed 56 tissue/sample values from PGB FASTA headers for 32,534 unique Arabidopsis genes. Computed pairwise tissue Spearman correlations, per-gene cross-tissue CV, and agreement between tissue-averaged high/low labels and each tissue’s median-based high/low label.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 AgroNT outperforms the CNN on a unified Arabidopsis benchmark

On the held-out PGB test set (*n* = 3,402), AgroNT reached 86.5% accuracy (AUROC 0.935, AUPRC 0.929), compared with 75.4% accuracy (AUROC 0.842, AUPRC 0.835) for the CNN (Table 1, Figure 3). The AUROC difference of 0.093 had a bootstrap 95% CI of 0.083–0.104, and McNemar's test was highly significant (*p* = 6.5×10⁻⁴⁷; AgroNT correct on 550 more genes and incorrect on 172 fewer genes than the CNN). These results show that plant-genome pretraining plus LoRA fine-tuning yields a large and statistically supported gain under identical splits and metrics.

**Table 1.** Unified benchmark on the PGB Arabidopsis test set.

| Model | Trainable params | Accuracy | AUROC | AUPRC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1D-CNN (DeepSEA-style) | 2.7 M | 75.4% | 0.842 | 0.835 |
| AgroNT-1B (LoRA) | 16.2 M (of 985 M) | **86.5%** | **0.935** | **0.929** |

### 3.2 Tissue-averaged labels capture a shared expression axis

Across 32,534 genes and 56 tissues/samples, pairwise tissue–tissue Spearman correlations were high (median 0.851; IQR 0.803–0.898), indicating substantial shared structure among expression profiles (Figure 7). Single-tissue high/low labels agreed with the tissue-averaged label for a mean of 92.1% of genes (range 78.7–95.4%). The median cross-tissue CV was 0.340, so tissue-dependent dispersion remains, but the dominant binary axis used for benchmarking is largely conserved across tissues. This analysis supports interpreting the present task as prediction of overall expression potential rather than organ-specific programs.

### 3.3 UTRs dominate CNN importance; AgroNT distributes attention more evenly

DeepLift attribution of the CNN ranked flanking regions by mean absolute importance per base as 5′UTR (0.0088) > 3′UTR (0.0081) > promoter (0.0048) > terminator (0.0038) (Figure 4). On a per-base basis, UTRs were ~1.8× more important than the promoter, consistent with prior reports that UTR sequence is highly predictive of expression level [peleke2024]. TF-MoDISco recovered four positive and four negative expression-associated patterns; TOMTOM against JASPAR 2024 mapped these predominantly to DOF-family factors (DOF1.5, DOF3.4/3.6, DOF5.1/5.8, CDF5), together with SPL8, NAC005, and AGL1. Sliding-window mutagenesis of AgroNT (50-bp windows) yielded more balanced promoter and UTR scores (promoter 0.553, 3′UTR 0.552, 5′UTR 0.536; terminator 0.404).

### 3.4 Predicted variant effects are highest in UTRs

Scoring 27,662 natural flanking-region SNVs mapped median |Δ| as 5′UTR (0.00269; *n*=2,542) > 3′UTR (0.00112; *n*=4,148) > terminator (0.00058; *n*=13,573) > promoter (0.00043; *n*=7,399) (Table 2, Figure 5b). Pooled UTR variants exceeded terminator variants (Mann–Whitney *p* = 4.3×10⁻⁹⁸) and promoter variants (*p* = 1.4×10⁻¹⁰⁶); 5′UTR exceeded 3′UTR (*p* = 1.2×10⁻⁶¹). Among 17 mapped eQTL-overlapping variants, median |Δ| was higher than the genome-wide background (0.00205 vs. 0.00065; exploratory Mann–Whitney *p* = 0.041).

**Table 2.** Variant effect magnitude by flanking region.

| Region | *n* | Median \|Δ\| | Mean \|Δ\| | Q95 \|Δ\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5′UTR | 2,542 | 0.00269 | 0.00676 | 0.0264 |
| 3′UTR | 4,148 | 0.00112 | 0.00376 | 0.0155 |
| Terminator | 13,573 | 0.00058 | 0.00247 | 0.0104 |
| Promoter | 7,399 | 0.00043 | 0.00177 | 0.0077 |

### 3.5 Cross-species transfer and input-length dependence

Without fine-tuning, the Arabidopsis-trained CNN predicted high-versus-low expression with AUROC 0.718 (rice; *n*=3,702), 0.689 (maize; *n*=4,483), 0.763 (tomato; *n*=3,827), and 0.763 (soybean; *n*=4,803) (Figure 6, Table 3). Dicots transferred better than monocots. Truncating the 6-kb PGB window to 3 kb or 1.5 kb collapsed CNN performance nearly to chance (AUROC 0.556 and 0.541 vs. 0.842), whereas the structured ~3 kb flanking representation retained test AUROC 0.825.

**Table 3.** Zero-shot cross-species transfer.

| Species | *n*_test | Accuracy | AUROC | AUPRC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rice | 3,702 | 0.646 | 0.718 | 0.709 |
| Maize | 4,483 | 0.585 | 0.689 | 0.681 |
| Tomato | 3,827 | 0.675 | 0.763 | 0.773 |
| Soybean | 4,803 | 0.666 | 0.763 | 0.747 |

## 4. Discussion

**Architecture choice.** +0.093 AUROC (95% CI 0.083–0.104; McNemar *p* = 6.5×10⁻⁴⁷) at LoRA cost (1.6% trainable parameters).

**What the models learn.** UTR-centered predictive associations; DOF-family motif enrichment; region-resolved variant effects congruent with attribution.

**Tissue-averaged scope.** Median tissue–tissue Spearman 0.851; mean label concordance 92.1% (min 78.7%). Residual disagreement marks the boundary for organ-specific programs [peleke2024].

**Cross-species.** AUROC 0.69–0.76; dicots > monocots; below within-species performance.

**Limitations.** Few AgroNT epochs; CNN-centric DeepLift; eQTL *n*=17 exploratory; single primary species.

## 5. Conclusions

Under tissue-averaged PGB labels, AgroNT outperforms a DeepSEA-style CNN by a large, statistically supported margin. Predictive importance and variant effects concentrate in UTRs; tissue-averaged labels align with most single-tissue binary axes; and the CNN transfers across crops without fine-tuning.
