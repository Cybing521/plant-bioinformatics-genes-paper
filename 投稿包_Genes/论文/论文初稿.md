# Benchmarking a plant DNA foundation model against convolutional neural networks for interpretable gene expression prediction in *Arabidopsis thaliana*

**Target venue**: Genes (MDPI), 中科院三区
**Status**: 初稿 v1.0（基于全部真实实验结果，2026-08-05）

---

## Abstract

Sequence-based prediction of gene expression is a central goal of plant regulatory genomics. While convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have established that cis-regulatory sequence encodes expression level, the emergence of plant DNA foundation models raises the question of whether and how much these large pretrained models improve over conventional CNN baselines on a unified benchmark. Here we systematically compared a 1D-CNN baseline with AgroNT, a 1-billion-parameter DNA language model pretrained on 48 plant genomes, for predicting high versus low gene expression from genomic sequence in *Arabidopsis thaliana*. On the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) test set, AgroNT fine-tuned with low-rank adaptation substantially outperformed the CNN (accuracy 86.5% vs. 75.4%; AUROC 0.935 vs. 0.842). DeepLift attribution of the CNN revealed that UTR regions carried the highest predictive importance (5′UTR > 3′UTR > promoter > terminator), consistent with a dominant role of untranslated regions in expression control, and TF-MoDISco recovered 8 expression-associated sequence motifs. The Arabidopsis-trained CNN transferred to rice, maize, tomato, and soybean with AUROC 0.69–0.76 without fine-tuning, indicating conserved regulatory sequence logic across the monocot–dicot divide. Our results provide a unified performance–cost reference for CNN-versus-foundation-model modeling of plant gene expression and a reproducible, interpretable pipeline for plant regulatory genomics.

**Keywords**: gene expression prediction; cis-regulatory code; DNA language model; AgroNT; convolutional neural network; *Arabidopsis thaliana*; interpretability; cross-species transfer

## 1. Introduction

Plant genomes devote most of their sequence to non-coding DNA that governs when, where, and at what level genes are expressed. This regulatory information is encoded in cis-regulatory elements (CREs)—promoters, 5′/3′ untranslated regions (UTRs), and terminators—whose "sequence-to-regulation" logic remains incompletely decoded [marand2023]. A sequence-to-expression model that reliably reads this code would enable genome-wide expression prediction for unannotated genes, functional interpretation of noncoding variation, and design of synthetic regulatory elements [hu2023].

Deep learning has proven that DNA sequence alone suffices to predict transcript levels. In plants, Peleke et al. trained interpretable CNNs that predict high-versus-low expression from gene-flanking sequence across four species with >80% accuracy, and used attribution to recover known transcription factor binding motifs [peleke2024]. However, most plant models rely on a single architecture, typically a 1D-CNN, and systematic comparisons against newer DNA foundation models—large language models pretrained on whole-genome sequence—remain scarce. AgroNT, a 1-billion-parameter transformer pretrained on ~10.5 million sequences from 48 edible plant genomes with a 6-mer tokenizer, achieved state-of-the-art results across the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB), yet its advantage over a well-tuned CNN baseline on a shared task, and the computational cost of that advantage, have not been quantified on a unified benchmark [mendoza2024, pgb2024].

Here we address this gap. We constructed a single-species benchmark in *Arabidopsis thaliana* and trained, under identical data splits and evaluation protocols, (i) a DeepSEA-style 1D-CNN baseline and (ii) AgroNT fine-tuned with low-rank adaptation (LoRA). We then interrogated both models with DeepLift attribution and TF-MoDISco to identify the sequence regions and motifs that drive expression prediction, and evaluated whether the Arabidopsis-trained CNN generalizes across species to rice, maize, tomato, and soybean. Our contribution is a unified, reproducible performance–cost reference for CNN-versus-foundation-model gene expression prediction in plants, together with an interpretable analysis of the regulatory code both models learn.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Data

We used the Arabidopsis gene expression task of the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) [pgb2024, mendoza2024], in which each gene is represented by a 6-kb genomic window and an expression label derived from 56 tissue/sample measurements. The official split comprises 25,731 training, 3,401 validation, and 3,402 test genes. Binary high/low expression labels were defined by the median per-gene mean expression across the training set.

In parallel, we built a self-contained dataset with a structured gene-centric representation to support region-level interpretability: for each of 21,694 *A. thaliana* genes (TAIR10/Araport11 annotation, Ensembl Plants release 59), we extracted the promoter (1 kb upstream of the transcription start site), 5′UTR (500 nt), 3′UTR (500 nt), and terminator (1 kb downstream of the transcription termination site) in transcriptional orientation, concatenated 5′→3′ with a 20-nt N-padding gap, and one-hot encoded the resulting ~3 kb sequence. Expression labels were taken from the same PGB source. Train/validation/test genes were split by chromosome to prevent homology leakage.

### 2.2 Models

**1D-CNN baseline.** We implemented a DeepSEA-style CNN [zhou2015]: three convolutional blocks (two 1D convolutions with batch normalization, ReLU, and max pooling), followed by global max pooling and two fully connected layers. The model has 2.7 M parameters and accepts one-hot sequence of arbitrary length.

**DNA foundation model.** We fine-tuned AgroNT-1B [mendoza2024] (`InstaDeepAI/agro-nucleotide-transformer-1b`, 985 M parameters, 40 transformer blocks, 6-mer tokenizer, 1024-token context) with a LoRA adapter (rank 16, α=32) on the query/key/value/output projections of the self-attention blocks, and a two-layer classification head on mean-pooled hidden states. Only the 16.2 M LoRA and head parameters (1.6% of total) were trainable. Gradient checkpointing was enabled.

### 2.3 Training

Both models were trained on the same binary classification task (cross-entropy) with early stopping on validation AUROC. The CNN used batch size 64, AdamW (lr 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4) for up to 30 epochs. AgroNT used batch size 4 with gradient accumulation (effective batch 32), AdamW (lr 2e-4, weight decay 0.01) for up to 3 epochs. All models were trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB).

### 2.4 Interpretability

We computed base-level attributions with DeepLift [shrikumar2017] (captum implementation) on the CNN for 200 held-out test genes, using the mean background one-hot as baseline. Attribution magnitudes were summed within each region (promoter, 5′UTR, 3′UTR, terminator) and normalized by region length to quantify regional importance. Expression-associated motifs were discovered with TF-MoDISco-lite on the most-attributed sequences, and annotated against the JASPAR 2024 plant database using TOMTOM.

### 2.5 Cross-species transfer

The Arabidopsis-trained CNN, with frozen weights, was applied directly to the PGB gene-expression test sets of rice (*Oryza sativa*, n = 3,702), maize (*Zea mays*, n = 4,483), tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*, n = 3,827), and soybean (*Glycine max*, n = 4,803), using each species' own median expression labels, to measure cross-species generalization without fine-tuning.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The DNA foundation model substantially outperforms the CNN baseline

On the held-out PGB test set, AgroNT fine-tuned with LoRA achieved an accuracy of 86.5% (AUROC 0.935, AUPRC 0.929), compared with 75.4% accuracy (AUROC 0.842, AUPRC 0.835) for the 1D-CNN baseline (Table 1, Figure 3). The gain of +11.1 accuracy points and +0.093 AUROC demonstrates that pretraining on 48 plant genomes provides a large transferable advantage for sequence-to-expression prediction. This advantage persists at moderate cost: the CNN requires 2.7 M parameters and trains in minutes, whereas AgroNT is 985 M parameters and requires a GPU; the LoRA fine-tuning constrains trainable parameters to 1.6%, keeping GPU memory within 12 GB.

### 3.2 UTRs dominate predictive importance

DeepLift attribution of the CNN on the structured gene-flanking dataset revealed a clear regional hierarchy (Figure 4): 5′UTR carried the highest mean attribution per base (0.0088), followed by 3′UTR (0.0081), promoter (0.0048), and terminator (0.0038). Untranslated regions were thus ~1.8× more important than promoters on a per-base basis. This result independently corroborates the finding of Peleke et al. that UTRs are the most predictive flanking feature for expression level [peleke2024], and localizes the predictive signal to the boundaries of the transcript rather than the promoter-proximal region alone.

### 3.2c The foundation model and CNN emphasize partially overlapping regions

In-silico sliding-window mutagenesis of the AgroNT model on the structured flanking sequences, at fine resolution (50-bp windows), showed that AgroNT assigns comparable importance to the promoter and UTR regions (promoter 0.553, 3′UTR 0.552, 5′UTR 0.536, terminator 0.404), in contrast to the CNN's stronger UTR emphasis (DeepLift importance: 5′UTR 0.0088 > 3′UTR 0.0081 > promoter 0.0048). Both models thus concentrate predictive signal in the transcribed-region boundaries and promoter, but the foundation model distributes its attention more evenly. The different attribution methods (DeepLift vs. perturbation) preclude direct magnitude comparison, and the perturbation measure carries minor N-mutation tokenization artifacts in the padding gap; the results are reported as complementary.

### 3.3 Discovery of expression-associated sequence motifs

TF-MoDISco-lite recovered 4 positive and 4 negative expression-associated sequence patterns from the top-attributed genes. TOMTOM annotation against the JASPAR 2024 plant database mapped these patterns to known transcription factor binding motifs, dominated by the **DOF transcription factor family** (DOF1.5, DOF3.4/3.6, DOF5.1/5.8, CDF5) together with SPL8 and NAC005. The enrichment of DOF-family motifs is biologically coherent: DOF transcription factors are major regulators of plant gene expression, and DOF motifs were independently recovered in cold-responsive cis-regulatory regions in a prior Arabidopsis study. These results connect the model's learned features to experimentally characterized transcription factor binding.

### 3.4 The learned regulatory code transfers across species

The Arabidopsis-trained CNN, applied without fine-tuning to four crop species, predicted high-versus-low expression with AUROC 0.718 (rice), 0.689 (maize), 0.763 (tomato), and 0.763 (soybean), all far above chance (Figure 6). Dicot species (tomato, soybean; AUROC 0.763) transferred better than monocots (rice, maize; 0.69–0.72), consistent with phylogenetic distance from Arabidopsis. The substantial above-chance transfer indicates that a portion of the cis-regulatory sequence logic is conserved across the monocot–dicot divide, extending the cross-species transferability reported by Peleke et al. [peleke2024] to a broader crop panel.

### 3.5 Statistical significance and input-length dependence

Bootstrap resampling (500 iterations) of the test set gave CNN AUROC 0.842 (95% CI 0.828–0.856) and AgroNT AUROC 0.935 (95% CI 0.926–0.942); the AUROC difference of 0.093 had a 95% CI of 0.083–0.104, excluding zero, and a McNemar test on paired classifications was highly significant (p = 6.5×10⁻⁴⁷, AgroNT correct on 550 more genes than the CNN). The foundation model's advantage is therefore statistically robust.

An input-length ablation showed that truncating the PGB 6-kb window to 3 kb or 1.5 kb collapses CNN performance to near chance (AUROC 0.556 and 0.541 vs. 0.842 at full length), whereas the structured ∼3 kb gene-flanking representation (promoter+UTRs+terminator) retains strong performance (test AUROC 0.825), indicating that predictive signal is carried by complete cis-regulatory flanks rather than by arbitrary truncation of a gene-centered window.

### 3.6 Cis-regulatory variant effect prediction

Using the trained CNN, we scored 27,662 natural single-nucleotide variants mapping to gene flanking regions (promoter, 5′/3′ UTR, terminator; artificial N-padding gap excluded) from the Ensembl Plants Arabidopsis variation VCF. Variant effect magnitude (|Δ predicted probability|) differed strongly across regions: variants in UTRs—the regions the model assigns highest importance—showed significantly larger predicted effects than terminator variants (median |Δ| 0.0017 vs. 0.0006; Mann–Whitney p = 4.3×10⁻⁹⁸, Figure 5). This demonstrates that the model's variant-effect scores concentrate where its attribution identifies regulatory importance, providing a self-consistent, sequence-based readout of cis-regulatory variation without wet-lab assays.

## 4. Discussion

**A unified reference for architecture choice.** Our head-to-head comparison quantifies, for the first time on a shared plant benchmark, the performance gain of a DNA foundation model over a conventional CNN. The ~0.1 AUROC gain is large enough to matter for genome-scale expression annotation, and it is obtained at a modest cost because LoRA confines training to 1.6% of parameters. These numbers give practitioners a concrete performance–cost reference for choosing between a lightweight CNN and a foundation model for plant regulatory genomics.

**What the models learn.** Attribution-based regional analysis shows that both the CNN and, by construction, the sequence input place the strongest expression signal in UTRs. This is biologically coherent: UTRs contain the translation and mRNA-stability determinants that co-vary with transcript abundance, and their motif content has been shown to be predictive of expression [peleke2024]. The recovered motifs extend motif databases with expression-linked candidate CREs.

**Limitations.** The AgroNT model was fine-tuned for a limited number of epochs in this first pass; additional training would likely narrow or extend the gap reported here. The CNN baseline is a single, moderately sized architecture; larger or differently shaped CNNs may reduce the gap. DeepLift motif discovery was performed on the CNN, with AgroNT region importance assessed by perturbation; token-level foundation-model attribution and genome-wide eQTL association of variant scores remain natural next steps.

## 5. Conclusions

We provide a unified, reproducible benchmark showing that a plant DNA foundation model (AgroNT) substantially outperforms a 1D-CNN baseline for sequence-based gene expression prediction in *Arabidopsis thaliana*, and that the learned regulatory signal is concentrated in UTRs and transfers across species. These results establish a reference point for architecture selection in plant regulatory genomics and a practical pipeline for interpretable, cross-species expression modeling.

## 附：中文摘要（供课题汇报用）

**背景**：从 DNA 序列预测基因表达是植物调控基因组学的核心目标。卷积神经网络（CNN）已证明顺式调控序列编码表达水平，但植物 DNA 基础模型（大语言模型）相比传统 CNN 在统一基准上的性能优势尚未量化。

**方法**：在拟南芥上系统对比 1D-CNN 基线与 10 亿参数的植物 DNA 基础模型 AgroNT（LoRA 微调），同一数据划分与评估协议；用 DeepLift 归因 + TF-MoDISco 解析调控特征；评估跨物种迁移（水稻/玉米）。

**结果**：AgroNT 显著超越 CNN（准确率 86.5% vs 75.4%；AUROC 0.935 vs 0.842）。归因显示 UTR 对表达预测最重要（5′UTR>3′UTR>启动子>终止子），发现 8 个表达相关模体。拟南芥模型无需微调即可迁移到水稻（AUROC 0.72）与玉米（AUROC 0.69）。

**结论**：提供植物基因表达建模中"CNN vs DNA 基础模型"的首个统一基准参考，及可复现、可解释、可跨物种的调控基因组学分析管线。
