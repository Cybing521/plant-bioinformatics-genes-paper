\abstract{\textbf{Background/Objectives:} Sequence-based gene expression prediction is central to plant regulatory genomics, but practitioners lack a shared benchmark for choosing between compact convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and plant DNA foundation models. We quantified this trade-off in \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana} under matched data and evaluation protocols. \textbf{Methods:} We compared a DeepSEA-style 1D-CNN with AgroNT (1B parameters; LoRA fine-tuning) on the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) high-versus-low expression task. Labels were derived from tissue-averaged expression across 56 tissues/samples. We assessed regional importance (DeepLift; sliding-window mutagenesis), motif recovery (TF-MoDISco/TOMTOM), natural flanking-region variant effects, zero-shot crop transfer, input-length ablation, and tissue-label concordance supporting the averaged-label design. \textbf{Results:} AgroNT outperformed the CNN (accuracy 86.5\% vs.\ 75.4\%; AUROC 0.935 vs.\ 0.842; AUROC difference bootstrap 95\% CI 0.083--0.104; McNemar $p = 6.5\times10^{-47}$). CNN attributions ranked 5$'$UTR $>$ 3$'$UTR $>$ promoter $>$ terminator; recovered motifs were enriched for DOF-family factors. UTR variants showed larger predicted effects than terminator or promoter variants. Single-tissue high/low labels agreed with the tissue-averaged label for a mean of 92.1\% of genes (range 78.7--95.4\%). The Arabidopsis CNN transferred to rice, maize, tomato, and soybean with AUROC 0.69--0.76 without fine-tuning. \textbf{Conclusions:} Under tissue-averaged expression labels, AgroNT provides a large, statistically supported gain over a strong CNN baseline at moderate LoRA cost, with interpretable proximal cis-regulatory signal concentrated in UTRs.}

\section{Introduction}
Most of a plant genome is non-coding sequence that determines when, where, and at what level genes are expressed through cis-regulatory elements (CREs) in promoters, 5$'$/3$'$ untranslated regions (UTRs), and terminators \cite{marand2023}. Sequence-to-expression models that recover this code can support genome-wide annotation of uncharacterized genes, prioritization of noncoding variants, and design of synthetic regulatory DNA \cite{hu2023}.

Deep learning has shown that proximal DNA sequence alone can predict transcript abundance. In plants, Peleke et al.\ trained interpretable CNNs that classify high-versus-low expression from gene-flanking sequence across four species with $>$80\% accuracy and recovered known transcription-factor motifs by attribution \cite{peleke2024}. Parallel work has introduced plant DNA foundation models---large language models pretrained on whole-genome sequence. AgroNT, a one-billion-parameter transformer pretrained on $\sim$10.5 million sequences from 48 edible plant genomes, reported strong results across the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) \cite{mendoza2024,pgb2024}. What remains unclear for applied regulatory genomics is not whether either architecture can work, but how large the gain of foundation-model fine-tuning is relative to a well-tuned CNN under identical splits, metrics, and compute constraints, and which sequence compartments drive that prediction.

Here we provide that comparison in \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana}. Under matched data and evaluation protocols, we train (i) a DeepSEA-style 1D-CNN baseline and (ii) AgroNT fine-tuned with low-rank adaptation (LoRA). We then quantify regional importance and motif content with DeepLift and TF-MoDISco, score natural flanking-region variants for predicted expression effects, measure concordance between tissue-averaged and single-tissue labels, and test zero-shot transfer of the Arabidopsis CNN to rice, maize, tomato, and soybean. The contribution is a reproducible performance--cost reference for CNN-versus-foundation-model expression prediction in plants, together with an interpretability and transfer analysis under tissue-averaged expression labels.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Data}
We used the \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana} gene-expression task of the Plants Genomic Benchmark (PGB) \cite{pgb2024,mendoza2024}. Each gene is represented by a 6-kb genomic window centered on the gene, with an expression label derived from 56 tissue/sample RNA-sequencing measurements. The official split comprises 25{,}731 training, 3{,}401 validation, and 3{,}402 test genes, with no gene overlap across splits. Binary high/low labels were defined by the median of per-gene mean expression on the training set, yielding a balanced two-class problem. Because labels aggregate expression across 56 tissues/samples, the benchmark targets cis-regulatory determinants of overall (tissue-averaged) expression potential rather than organ- or tissue-specific regulatory programs.

In parallel, we built a structured gene-centric dataset for region-level interpretability. For each of 21{,}694 protein-coding \emph{A.\ thaliana} genes (TAIR10/Araport11 annotation, Ensembl Plants release 59), we extracted the promoter (1 kb upstream of the transcription start site), 5$'$UTR (500 nt), 3$'$UTR (500 nt), and terminator (1 kb downstream of the transcription termination site) in transcriptional orientation, concatenated the four regions 5$'$$\rightarrow$3$'$ with a 20-nt N-padding gap, and one-hot encoded the resulting $\sim$3 kb sequence. Genes were assigned to training/validation/test splits by chromosome (chromosomes 5 and 3, 4, and 1 and 2, respectively) to reduce sequence-homology leakage between splits.

\subsection{Model Architectures}
\textbf{1D-CNN baseline.} We implemented a DeepSEA-style convolutional network \cite{zhou2015} with three convolutional blocks, each containing two 1D convolutions (256 filters, kernel size 8), batch normalization, ReLU activation, and max pooling (pool size 4), followed by global max pooling and two fully connected layers with dropout. The model has 2.7 M parameters and accepts one-hot DNA of arbitrary length; global max pooling makes the classification head independent of input length.

\textbf{DNA foundation model.} AgroNT-1B \cite{mendoza2024} is a transformer of 985 M parameters (40 layers, hidden size 1500) pretrained on $\sim$10.5 million sequences from 48 edible plant genomes with a 6-mer tokenizer and a 1024-token context. We adapted it for sequence classification with LoRA adapters (rank 16, $\alpha=32$) on the query/key/value/output projections of every self-attention block, plus a two-layer head over mean-pooled hidden states. Only the 16.2 M LoRA and head parameters (1.6\% of the total) were trainable; gradient checkpointing kept peak GPU memory within 12 GB.

\subsection{Training and Evaluation}
Both models were trained with cross-entropy loss and early stopping on validation AUROC (patience of 2--5 epochs). The CNN used batch size 64 and AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) for up to 30 epochs. AgroNT used batch size 4 with gradient accumulation over 8 steps (effective batch size 32) and AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$) for up to 3 epochs. All training used a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB). Reported metrics were accuracy, AUROC, and AUPRC.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
We assessed the CNN--AgroNT difference with 500-fold bootstrap resampling of the test set to obtain 95\% confidence intervals for each AUROC and for the AUROC difference; intervals excluding zero were treated as statistically supported. We additionally applied McNemar's test to paired correct/incorrect classifications. An input-length ablation retrained the CNN on truncated PGB windows of 3 kb and 1.5 kb. Performance of the structured $\sim$3 kb flanking representation was evaluated on its held-out chromosomal test split.

\subsection{Interpretability}
Base-level attributions for the CNN were computed with DeepLift \cite{shrikumar2017} (captum) on 200 held-out test genes, using the mean background one-hot vector as baseline. Absolute attributions were summed within each flanking region and normalized by region length. Expression-associated motifs were discovered with TF-MoDISco-lite on the most-attributed sequences and annotated against JASPAR 2024 plant motifs with TOMTOM. For AgroNT, region importance was estimated by in-silico sliding-window mutagenesis (50-bp windows, 25-bp step) on the structured flanking sequences.

\subsection{Tissue Heterogeneity of Expression Labels}
To characterize the biological scope of tissue-averaged labels, we parsed the 56 tissue/sample expression values encoded in PGB FASTA headers for 32{,}534 unique Arabidopsis genes. We computed pairwise Spearman correlations among tissues, the per-gene cross-tissue coefficient of variation (CV), and the agreement between the tissue-averaged high/low label (thresholded at the training-set median of gene means) and each tissue's own median-based high/low label.

\subsection{Cross-Species Transfer}
The Arabidopsis-trained CNN, with frozen weights, was applied to the PGB gene-expression test sets of rice (\emph{Oryza sativa}), maize (\emph{Zea mays}), tomato (\emph{Solanum lycopersicum}), and soybean (\emph{Glycine max}), using each species' own median expression labels, to measure zero-shot transfer without fine-tuning.

\subsection{Variant Effect Prediction}
Natural single-nucleotide variants from the Ensembl Plants \emph{A.\ thaliana} variation VCF were mapped onto the structured flanking representation (promoter, 5$'$/3$'$ UTR, terminator), excluding the artificial N-padding gap, yielding 27{,}662 scored variants. For each variant, the reference flanking sequence was mutated in silico to the alternate allele and re-scored by the CNN; the predicted effect was the absolute change in expression probability ($|\Delta|$). We compared $|\Delta|$ across flanking regions with Mann--Whitney $U$ tests. As an exploratory check, 17 variants overlapping reported eQTL associations were contrasted with the genome-wide scored background.

\section{Results}
\subsection{AgroNT Outperforms the CNN on a Unified Arabidopsis Benchmark}
On the held-out PGB test set ($n = 3{,}402$), AgroNT reached 86.5\% accuracy (AUROC 0.935, AUPRC 0.929), compared with 75.4\% accuracy (AUROC 0.842, AUPRC 0.835) for the CNN (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}; Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark}). The AUROC difference of 0.093 had a bootstrap 95\% CI of 0.083--0.104, and McNemar's test was highly significant ($p = 6.5\times10^{-47}$; AgroNT correct on 550 more genes and incorrect on 172 fewer genes than the CNN). These results show that plant-genome pretraining plus LoRA fine-tuning yields a large and statistically supported gain under identical splits and metrics.

\subsection{Tissue-Averaged Labels Capture a Shared Expression Axis}
Across 32{,}534 genes and 56 tissues/samples, pairwise tissue--tissue Spearman correlations were high (median 0.851; IQR 0.803--0.898), indicating substantial shared structure among expression profiles (Figure~\ref{fig:tissue}). Single-tissue high/low labels agreed with the tissue-averaged label for a mean of 92.1\% of genes (range 78.7--95.4\%). The median cross-tissue CV was 0.340, so tissue-dependent dispersion remains, but the dominant binary axis used for benchmarking is largely conserved across tissues. This analysis supports interpreting the present task as prediction of overall expression potential rather than organ-specific programs.

\subsection{UTRs Dominate CNN Importance; AgroNT Distributes Attention More Evenly}
DeepLift attribution of the CNN ranked flanking regions by mean absolute importance per base as 5$'$UTR (0.0088) $>$ 3$'$UTR (0.0081) $>$ promoter (0.0048) $>$ terminator (0.0038) (Figure~\ref{fig:region}). On a per-base basis, UTRs were $\sim$1.8$\times$ more important than the promoter, consistent with prior reports that UTR sequence is highly predictive of expression level \cite{peleke2024}. TF-MoDISco recovered four positive and four negative expression-associated patterns; TOMTOM against JASPAR 2024 mapped these predominantly to DOF-family factors (DOF1.5, DOF3.4/3.6, DOF5.1/5.8, CDF5), together with SPL8, NAC005, and AGL1. Sliding-window mutagenesis of AgroNT (50-bp windows) yielded more balanced promoter and UTR scores (promoter 0.553, 3$'$UTR 0.552, 5$'$UTR 0.536; terminator 0.404). Because DeepLift and perturbation quantify importance differently, magnitudes are not directly comparable; together they indicate that predictive signal concentrates in proximal cis-regulatory compartments.

\subsection{Predicted Variant Effects Are Highest in UTRs}
Scoring 27{,}662 natural flanking-region SNVs mapped median $|\Delta|$ as 5$'$UTR (0.00269; $n=2{,}542$) $>$ 3$'$UTR (0.00112; $n=4{,}148$) $>$ terminator (0.00058; $n=13{,}573$) $>$ promoter (0.00043; $n=7{,}399$) (Table~\ref{tab:variant}; Figure~\ref{fig:variant}). Pooled UTR variants exceeded terminator variants (Mann--Whitney $p = 4.3\times10^{-98}$) and promoter variants ($p = 1.4\times10^{-106}$); 5$'$UTR exceeded 3$'$UTR ($p = 1.2\times10^{-61}$). Thus variant-effect magnitude concentrates where attribution identifies regulatory importance. Among 17 mapped eQTL-overlapping variants, median $|\Delta|$ was higher than the genome-wide background (0.00205 vs.\ 0.00065; exploratory Mann--Whitney $p = 0.041$), a directional but small-sample check.

\subsection{Cross-Species Transfer and Input-Length Dependence}
Without fine-tuning, the Arabidopsis-trained CNN predicted high-versus-low expression with AUROC 0.718 (rice; $n=3{,}702$), 0.689 (maize; $n=4{,}483$), 0.763 (tomato; $n=3{,}827$), and 0.763 (soybean; $n=4{,}803$) (Figure~\ref{fig:cross}; Table~\ref{tab:cross}). Dicots transferred better than monocots, consistent with phylogenetic distance from \emph{Arabidopsis}. Truncating the 6-kb PGB window to 3 kb or 1.5 kb collapsed CNN performance nearly to chance (AUROC 0.556 and 0.541 vs.\ 0.842 at full length), whereas the structured $\sim$3 kb flanking representation retained test AUROC 0.825. Predictive information therefore depends on complete cis-regulatory flanks rather than on arbitrary truncation of a gene-centered window.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Architecture choice under a shared plant benchmark.} Head-to-head evaluation on PGB Arabidopsis quantifies the gain of a plant DNA foundation model over a DeepSEA-style CNN ($+0.093$ AUROC; bootstrap 95\% CI 0.083--0.104; McNemar $p = 6.5\times10^{-47}$). LoRA confines training to 1.6\% of AgroNT parameters and keeps GPU memory within 12 GB, so the gain comes at moderate fine-tuning cost relative to full-model updates. For genome-scale annotation where an $\sim$0.1 AUROC improvement is material, fine-tuned AgroNT is preferable; where compute is limiting, the 2.7 M-parameter CNN remains a strong baseline. The length ablation further shows that truncating the gene-centered 6-kb window destroys performance, whereas a structured $\sim$3 kb flank retains AUROC 0.825, indicating that predictive signal is organized in complete proximal cis-regulatory compartments.

\textbf{What the models learn.} Both models concentrate predictive signal in transcribed-region boundaries and the promoter, with the CNN emphasizing UTRs and AgroNT distributing importance more evenly across promoter and UTRs. This pattern is biologically coherent: UTRs harbor determinants of transcript stability and translation that co-vary with mRNA abundance, and DOF-family motif enrichment recovered by TF-MoDISco is consistent with known roles of DOF factors in plant transcriptional control \cite{peleke2024}. Region-resolved variant scores provide a congruent readout (5$'$UTR $>$ 3$'$UTR $>$ terminator/promoter). Attribution and variant analyses should be read as predictive associations, not as causal maps of regulatory mechanism.

\textbf{Tissue-averaged scope.} High tissue--tissue correlation (median Spearman 0.851) and high concordance between single-tissue and averaged high/low labels (mean 92.1\%) indicate that the benchmark captures a shared expression axis across most tissues. Residual disagreement in a minority of tissues (agreement as low as 78.7\%) marks the boundary of this design: organ-specific programs are not the prediction target and remain better addressed with tissue-conditioned labels and distal/epigenomic context \cite{peleke2024}.

\textbf{Cross-species conservation.} Zero-shot transfer AUROC of 0.69--0.76 across four crops, with stronger dicot than monocot transfer, indicates that part of the proximal cis-regulatory signature learned in \emph{Arabidopsis} is conserved across the monocot--dicot divide. This extends earlier cross-species analyses \cite{peleke2024} to a broader crop panel. Transfer remains below within-species performance, so crop-specific fine-tuning would still be expected to improve accuracy.

\textbf{Limitations.} AgroNT was fine-tuned for few epochs; DeepLift motif discovery was performed on the CNN, with AgroNT region importance assessed by perturbation rather than token-level attribution; eQTL overlap was exploratory ($n=17$); and primary training was confined to one model species despite the transfer experiments. Extending the benchmark to tissue-of-expression prediction, integrating accessibility or methylation features \cite{takou2025}, expanding eQTL validation, and evaluating longer-context sequence models \cite{avsec2021} are natural next steps.

\section{Conclusions}
Under a unified Arabidopsis PGB task with tissue-averaged labels, AgroNT fine-tuned with LoRA outperforms a DeepSEA-style 1D-CNN by a large and statistically supported margin. Predictive importance and variant effects concentrate in UTRs, recovered motifs are enriched for DOF-family factors, tissue-averaged labels align with most single-tissue binary axes, and the CNN transfers across crop species without fine-tuning. These results supply a concrete performance--cost reference for architecture selection in plant sequence-to-expression modeling.
